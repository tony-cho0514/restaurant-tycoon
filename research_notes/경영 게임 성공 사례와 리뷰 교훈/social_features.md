# Social features in casual management/sim games: friend profiles, friend leaderboards, shop visits, async comparison & sharing

Scope note: Research done 2026-10-30 for a planned "compare shop assets with friends" feature in a solo, deterministic pizza-shop PWA (about 100 Korean med-student friends, no login, nickname only, no economic interaction between shops). All dates of historical games are labelled. Quotes are kept under 15 words; everything else is paraphrase. Source quality is flagged inline: (W) = wiki/user-edited, (S) = only a search-result summary was available, not the full page, (3P) = third-party/SEO guide.

## Q1. Restaurant City (Playfish, Facebook, 2009–2012): why it was loved, why it shut down, what players missed

### Takeaway
Restaurant City (2009–2012) was built around your Facebook friends appearing inside your restaurant: they worked there as staff even if they did not play. It peaked at about 18M monthly users. It was shut down on 29 June 2012 after losing about 90% of its players, a decline that hit the whole Facebook-restaurant genre. Nothing points to a feature that backfired. The cause looks like a shrinking platform plus EA's struggles. Detailed evidence of "what players missed most" could not be retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Released 28 April 2009, retired 29 June 2012. Peak was 18M+ monthly active users, down to 1.8M by 2012. The core goal was to run a popular restaurant and raise restaurant level and dish quality. You need at least a chef and a waiter, and higher popularity brings more customers and so needs more staff. Levelling up enlarges the restaurant and unlocks drinks and a garden for growing ingredients. Nominated for "Social Networking Game of the Year" (13th Interactive Achievement Awards). — [Wikipedia: Playfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playfish) (W)
- Peak above 5.2M daily active users. By April 2012 (year 3) it had lost 90%+ of its peak users. Retention fell to 20% in September 2011. A big iOS update in October 2011 gave a brief rally, then numbers fell again in early 2012. The article frames this as a "general decline across the genre" and says Zynga's café game was also losing users. — [MCV/Develop, 1 May 2012](https://mcvuk.com/?p=97276). Note: MCV says EA bought Playfish "in 2010", but Wikipedia says the acquisition was announced 9 Nov 2009 for up to $400M ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playfish)).
- Shutdown handling: players were told to spend remaining Cooking Cash. Balances moved to The Sims Social as SimCash, plus 1,000 free Simoleons. — [Joystiq/AOL, 1 May 2012](https://www.aol.com/2012/05/01/restaurant-city-shut-down) (S)
- Fan legacy: by July 2012 at least 11 former players, all under 25, were building a tribute game, "Restaurant World". — [Joystiq/AOL, 4 Jul 2012](https://www.aol.com/2012/07/04/restaurant-world-preview) (S; the page now returns 404)
- Friend-as-staff mechanic: you hire friends as employees, and friends can work in your restaurant without playing the game themselves. — [AOL/Joystiq tips guide, 29 Dec 2009](https://www.aol.com/2009-12-29-restaurant-city-tips-and-cheats-getting-started-guide.html) (S; the page now returns 404)
- Playfish context: by February 2013 all four founders had left. EA retired Pet Society, The Sims Social and SimCity Social in June 2013. — [Wikipedia: Playfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playfish) (W)

### Inferences
- The emotional hook came from your friends' faces and names appearing in your own space (staff, customers). Comparing numbers was not the hook. Non-players could be "hired", so the social layer did not depend on friends being active. That matters for a 100-person group in which many will be inactive at any given time.
- The shutdown reflects platform and business decline (Facebook canvas games 2011–2012, EA's social unit), not a social feature that backfired. Do not read the closure as evidence that friend features fail.
- Fans organized to rebuild the game themselves, which suggests attachment to the game's identity and friend-filled restaurants more than to any leaderboard. This is inference only. Direct evidence of "what players missed" was not retrieved (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- The Restaurant City fandom wiki (HTTP 402) and the Lost Media Wiki page (HTTP 403) could not be read. So the details of ingredient gifting/trading, the weekly ingredient, "Restaurant Street" and friend rankings are unverified here.
- No Reddit or forum nostalgia thread ("I miss Restaurant City") turned up in search. What players said they missed most is therefore not sourced.
- No official statement of the shutdown reason was found beyond the platform-wide decline that MCV reported.

## Q2. Café World, FarmVille, Hay Day, Animal Crossing, My Cafe, Pokémon GO: which async social mechanics drove engagement, and were they fun or an obligation?

### Takeaway
Async mechanics that let friends show up in your world, or help without you needing them, were remembered fondly. Examples are Hay Day's 30-person neighborhoods and weekly derby, Animal Crossing's offline "dream" visits, and Pokémon GO's once-a-day gifts. Mechanics that locked progress behind friends, or pushed wall posts and requests (FarmVille's "social pyramid"), were widely criticized as spam and obligation.

### Cited Findings
**FarmVille (Zynga, 2009–)**
- A German academic article (Kinder, 2012) argues that social relationships are a main explanation for FarmVille's success. It notes that the largest player group was women over 35. — [SSOAR: "You have a Farmville gift request"](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/handle/document/28274); [Lancaster ePrints](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/52434) (S)
- Critics called it a "Social Pyramid Scheme" (attributed to Gray et al., catalogue entry dated 2 Aug 2014). The game pushed invites because some features and goals were useless or locked unless friends also played. The catalogue says its examples are not independently verified. — [Deceptive Patterns: FarmVille friend spam](https://deceptive.design/articles/farmville-friend-spam) (S)
- Some content was locked unless enough friends played. Friends were a reliable source of coins, XP and gifts. A Facebook prompt to post and invite friends appeared whenever you did something in the game. — [Game Developer: FarmVille, social gaming and addiction](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/farmville-social-gaming-and-addiction) (S)
- The Globe and Mail described players as needing a set number of playing friends to progress, which turned players into "recruiters". — [Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/technology/when-gamers-become-recruiters/article4294737) (S). Counterpoint: PopMatters argues "pyramid game" fits better than "scheme" because there is no real deception. — [PopMatters](https://www.popmatters.com/123035-the-appeal-of-farmville-2496185604.html) (S). A Cornell course blog notes FarmVille and Mafia Wars made friend interaction optional for progression, whereas The Sims Social was the first to require it. — [Cornell INFO 2040 blog, 2011](https://blogs.cornell.edu/info2040/2011/10/10/2606) (S)
- Scams exploited FarmVille's gift culture: fake "rare animal" gifts led to surveys and malware (2011). — [Help Net Security, 20 Jan 2011](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2011/01/20/rare-animal-gift-scams-target-farmville-players/)

**Café World (Zynga, 2009–)**
- Only data point found: Zynga's café game was also losing users in the 2012 genre-wide decline. — [MCV, 1 May 2012](https://mcvuk.com/?p=97276)

**Hay Day (Supercell, 2012–)**
- Social elements, namely neighborhoods and neighborhood derbies, were added in 2014. A former game lead credits them with player retention. — [Wikipedia: Hay Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hay_Day) (W)
- A neighborhood holds up to 30 players, who exchange tips, trade goods and compete in weekly derby events. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/506627515) (S)
- The roadside shop is a player-to-player market where others browse and buy your goods. Connecting Facebook shows which friends play. — third-party guides, e.g. [Screenwise parent guide](https://screenwiseapp.com/guides/hay-day) (3P, S)

**Animal Crossing: New Horizons (Nintendo, 2020)**
- "Dream" visits let you tour another player's island even while they are not playing. You cannot change anything on the dream island. You need a Dream Address from Luna and Nintendo Switch Online. — [Nintendo Support](https://en-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/50094/~/how-to-visit-another-players-dream-island-%28animal-crossing%3A-new-horizons%29); [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/683468/how-to-visit-someones-island-in-dreams-in-animal-crossing-new-horizons/)
- Student commentary from the 2020 lockdown period: friends trading codes formed strong ties, while casual visitors formed weak ties. Students called the game a good shared activity, though not a replacement for meeting in person. — [Cornell INFO 2040 blog, 2020](https://blogs.cornell.edu/info2040/2020/09/29/animal-crossing-a-new-social-media); [Willamette Collegian](https://www.willamettecollegian.com/post/animal-crossing-how-willamette-students-are-using-the-beloved-quarantine-pastime-to-stay-connected) (non-peer-reviewed)

**Pokémon GO (Niantic; Friends added June 2018)**
- The Friends feature launched in June 2018 with gifting and trading. Gifts come from spinning PokéStops and Gyms. — [Pokémon.com news](https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokemon-news/friends-trading-and-gifts-come-to-pokemon-go)
- Friendship can rise at most once per day per friend, through a gift, trade or battle. Levels Good/Great/Ultra/Best need 1/7/30/90 days of interaction. — [Bulbapedia: Friends (GO)](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Friends_(GO)) (W)
- Gift limits differ across sources (1 per friend per day, 20 opened per day, per Serebii; other sites give different figures). — [Serebii](https://serebii.net//pokemongo//friends.shtml) (3P)

**General**
- A practitioner blog on SimCity Social (2012) lists tactics that push players to spam friends, trade items and recruit others, and frames these as exploiting reciprocity. — [Psychology of Games](https://psychologyofgames.com/tag/reciprocity) (opinion)

### Inferences
- **Remembered as fun:** mechanics where a friend's presence is ambient and optional. Hay Day's derby is a cooperative weekly goal, and Animal Crossing's dream visits are view-only and need no host. Both reward showing up without forcing anyone to ask.
- **Felt as obligation or spam:** progress gated behind friend counts, auto-posted requests, and daily per-friend interactions that become chores (Pokémon GO's daily gift cap turns friendship into a streak; this is inference from the mechanic, not from user data found here).
- For a no-monetization game with about 100 friends, the FarmVille lesson is direct: never make any upgrade, recipe or neighborhood depend on friends.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found for My Cafe's towns/leagues mechanics or player sentiment about them.
- No peer-reviewed study directly measuring felt obligation from gift requests was found. Gray et al.'s paper itself was not retrieved.
- No Café World-specific social mechanics were sourced.

## Q3. Korean Kakao-era games (애니팡 2012, 아이러브커피 2012): how KakaoTalk friend lists and rankings drove them, and what fatigue they caused

### Takeaway
애니팡 (Anipang, July 2012) rode KakaoTalk friend lists to about 7M daily users within about 40 days. The engine was a weekly friend score ranking plus "heart" (life) exchange. It produced notorious "heart stress": notifications at midnight and dawn, and awkward hearts from exes and distant acquaintances. Kakao capped game-invite messages at once a month in January 2013. 아이러브커피 (I Love Coffee, Kakao version August 2012) lasted much longer. It was a slow, decorate-and-grow café sim whose social layer was visiting friends' cafés to "help" (품앗이) and friend-consent items for expansion. Its own fatigue came from grind (quests, decoration-score walls), not from rankings.

### Cited Findings
**애니팡 (SundayToz, Kakao 게임하기 launch 30 July 2012)**
- About 12M downloads in about 40 days, about 7M daily users, 2M+ concurrent (as of September 2012). A heart is spent per 1-minute game and recharges every 8 minutes. When out of hearts, you invite non-playing Kakao friends or exchange hearts with friends. — [머니투데이, 2012-09-22 「3년만에 받은 '하트'에 낚여(?) 줌마도 빠졌다」](https://www.mt.co.kr/tech/2012/09/22/2012092111361695742)
- Friend ranking effects (same article): the reporter's 100k weekly score versus the top Kakao friend's 390k. A boss ranking #1 among friends made a usually distant superior feel approachable.
- Fatigue (same article): many people were annoyed by heart notifications arriving at all hours, e.g. midnight and 6 a.m. The reporter got a heart from an acquaintance not contacted in 3 years and briefly read meaning into it. People agonized over how to respond to hearts from exes or not-close contacts. One parent regretted losing a weekend with their child to the game.
- Opt-outs: Anipang let players refuse hearts, and KakaoTalk's app-management settings could block app messages. A 35-year-old office worker blocked heart receiving because of "heart stress". — [매일신문, 2012-09-29](https://www.imaeil.com/page/view/2012092908273227181); [디스이즈게임](https://www.thisisgame.com/articles/36935) (S)
- Kakao platform response: from January 2013, game-invite messages to friends could be sent only once a month, because spam invites had become another stress for smartphone users. — [매일신문, 2013-01-19](https://www.imaeil.com/page/view/2013011908354515957) (S)
- Retrospective (2016): invite messages, once a word-of-mouth tool, came to be treated as "spam" by users. — [게임메카](https://www.gamemeca.com/news.php?p=3746) (S)

**아이러브커피 (Pati Games; Kakao version 2012-08-20)**
- Release history: Naver web version 2012-06-28 (web service ended 2017-04-06); Kakao mobile version 2012-08-20; LINE version March 2013. Members across Korea, China, Thailand and Taiwan passed 10M (cumulative) on 2012-10-21. The article contrasts its long-lived popularity with short-lived Kakao games like Anipang. Note: the article's intro also says "2011", which conflicts with its infobox. — [나무위키: 아이러브커피](https://namu.wiki/w/아이러브커피) (W)
- Social mechanics (same source): when you visit a friend's café you can do social actions (roasting beans, selling coffee, side production), which earn "coffee cherries" needed to build a second floor. Help is capped per level band and per friend (3–4 times). First-floor expansion requires "friend consent forms" (친구 동의서). Item gifting was added 2012-11-29, for cash items only. A dormant-account revival event ran 1–13 Feb 2013. No friend-ranking feature is described. — [나무위키](https://namu.wiki/w/아이러브커피) (W)
- Why it lasted (same source): a slow, steady management sim rather than a fast-cycle game, varied interior themes for self-expression, and realistic details (the developers reportedly observed a Hollys Coffee store in Jamsil). — [나무위키](https://namu.wiki/w/아이러브커피) (W)
- Fatigue and complaints (same source): quests pile up with every level and cannot be abandoned. Late-game decoration-score requirements (800–1,200) force players to place decor that clashes with their café's concept. Coffee cherries were scarce for the 2014 second-floor expansion. A 2022 10th-anniversary patch nerfed several systems and hurt sentiment. — [나무위키](https://namu.wiki/w/아이러브커피) (W)
- Pati Games' follow-up 아이러브파스타 for Kakao reached #1 among Kakao popular games with about 200k DAU. — [게임메카](https://www.gamemeca.com/view.php?gid=480211) (S)

### Inferences
- Anipang's ranking was powerful because it was a friend ranking: tiny, local, socially meaningful (your boss, your old friend). The same tight coupling to a messaging app turned every game action into a social message, and that produced the fatigue. The lesson for a KakaoTalk-link game is to use Kakao to join, not to notify.
- I Love Coffee's long life came from self-expression (themes, decoration) plus low-stakes helping visits. That fits a management sim much better than score races. Its friend-consent gating for expansion is a FarmVille-style dependency to avoid.
- The awkwardness of receiving hearts (from exes, bosses, estranged acquaintances) is a privacy and comfort cost that appears whenever a game auto-surfaces your whole contact graph. A small, explicit friend list (friend code or link) avoids it.

### Gaps
- No primary Korean press article was retrieved that describes 아이러브커피's friend-visit features in 2012. The details rely on 나무위키 (user-edited).
- No data was found on how many Anipang players turned off hearts or rankings.

## Q4. Friend leaderboards vs global leaderboards: what research says

### Takeaway
Theory and experiments agree that comparisons with a few similar, nearby people are the most motivating. Festinger's similarity hypothesis, Zell & Alicke's "local dominance" effect and Garcia & Tor's "N-effect" (fewer competitors means more competitive drive) all point that way. Leaderboards act like implicit difficult goals (Landers). But their effect depends on rank and person. Top performers gain, while low-ranked, low-competitiveness or anxious users can lose motivation and enjoyment. Wordle's spoiler-free grid shows a low-pressure alternative: sharing invites conversation without forcing a ranked comparison.

### Cited Findings
**Social comparison theory**
- Festinger (1954): when objective standards are absent or ambiguous, people compare with others, and they prefer similar others because these give more relevant information. Very dissimilar people are less likely comparison targets. Upward and downward comparison types were elaborated later by other researchers. — [Wikipedia: Social comparison theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_comparison_theory); [The Decision Lab: Similarity hypothesis](https://thedecisionlab.com/reference-guide/sociology/similarity-hypothesis)
- Local dominance effect (Zell & Alicke 2009, *Psychological Science*): comparisons with a few people influence self-evaluation more than larger, more representative samples. Local information can override much larger, more valid data. — [Univ. of Iowa CRISP paper (PDF)](https://crisp.org.uiowa.edu/sites/crisp.org.uiowa.edu/files/2020-04/20.5.pdf)
- A follow-up study found local comparisons have a stronger effect on both self-evaluations and task enjoyment (intrinsic motivation) than general comparisons. — [Social Influence 2017: "Local dominance effects on self-evaluations and intrinsic motivation"](https://www.ingentaconnect.com/content/routledg/usai/2017/00000016/00000005/art00006) (S). Giving general standing information reduces people's appetite for local comparison (Buckingham & Alicke 2012, cited in the [CRISP paper](https://crisp.org.uiowa.edu/sites/crisp.org.uiowa.edu/files/2020-04/20.5.pdf)) (S)
- N-effect (Garcia & Tor 2009, *Psychological Science*): more competitors can lower competitive motivation even when the odds stay the same. Students told they competed against 10 people finished a quiz in about 29 s, versus about 33 s for those told they competed against 100. The effect was mediated by social comparison and was stronger in people high in social comparison orientation. — [Garcia & Tor, PDF](https://public.websites.umich.edu/~smgarcia/pubs/n-effect.pdf); [Phys.org, March 2009](https://phys.org/news/2009-03-competitors-competition.html)

**Leaderboards in gamification**
- Landers, Bauer & Callan (*Computers in Human Behavior*, 2017): a leaderboard alone raised brainstorming performance to about the level of "difficult" or "impossible" assigned goals. Participants appeared to set implicit goals near the top of the board, which supports goal-setting theory as the mechanism. — [Univ. of Minnesota Experts](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/gamification-of-task-performance-with-leaderboards-a-goal-setting/)
- 2025 replication/extension (Kratochvíl, Lieberoth et al.): leaderboards raised quantitative performance but not qualitative performance. The effect on self-reported performance was stronger for experienced learners. — [Masaryk University publication record](https://www.muni.cz/en/research/publications/2495997) (S)
- Landers & Landers (2014, *Simulation & Gaming*): students given a leaderboard interacted with their project 29.61 more times on average than controls. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/simgam/v45y2014i6p769-785.html) (S)
- Hamari, Koivisto & Sarsa (2014) reviewed 24 empirical studies. Gamification generally had positive effects, but these depended heavily on context and on the users. — [Aalto University research portal](https://research.aalto.fi/fi/publications/does-gamification-work-a-literature-review-of-empirical-studies-o/)
- Huschens, Rothlauf & Rothe (HICSS 2019): providing a ranking increased motivation and also perceived pressure and social-comparison behavior. The effects were largely explained by individuals' social-comparison tendency. — [ScholarSpace, Univ. of Hawaiʻi](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/items/7e2be796-8a77-4ac2-bfe5-8b851f39af88) (S)
- Effects on low-ranked players: a Topcoder quasi-experiment found that relative (rank-based) scoring raised top performers' effort and lowered bottom performers' effort. — [LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/115983) (S; attribution from search summary, full text not verified). Preist et al. (2014) report that low scorers were often demotivated, though the citing dissertation cautions this is correlational. — [UMN Conservancy dissertation](https://conservancy.umn.edu/bitstreams/f4f85e78-b169-4d1e-9b21-533113feae28/download) (S)
- Anxiety: in a 2024 study of 1,389 Irish primary pupils using an unbounded leaderboard, maths-anxious pupils disliked the leaderboard more even after controlling for their rank. Enjoyment depended on both position and anxiety. — [University of Galway: "Not only a matter of position"](https://research.universityofgalway.ie/en/publications/not-only-amatter-ofposition-player-traits-which-influence-theexpe/) (S)
- Practitioner guidance (not empirical): leaderboards work when they offer hope, proximity and recoverability. Recommendations include micro-leaderboards and not showing beginners a wall of top players. — [Yu-kai Chou](https://yukaichou.com/gamification-study/why-most-leaderboards-fail-and-how-to-fix-yours-in-5-steps/)
- Duolingo first tested leaderboards in 2018 as an easy way for learners to benchmark progress. — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-leagues-leaderboards/). Third-party analyses describe weekly leagues of about 30 learners at a similar level, with promotion and demotion. — [Deconstructor of Fun: Duolingo leagues](https://duolingo.deconstructoroffun.com/mechanics/leagues) (3P; exact cut-offs vary by source)

**Wordle's share grid (Dec 2021) as a low-pressure model**
- On 16 Dec 2021 Wardle added a share button that generates a "spoiler-free emoji grid". He built it after seeing players hand-type green/yellow/black emoji grids. — [AP via Boston.com, 4 Jan 2022](https://www.boston.com/news/national-news/2022/01/04/he-made-wordle-for-his-partner-now-its-an-online-hit/); [WION](https://www.wionews.com/technology/he-made-wordle-for-his-partner-now-its-an-online-hit-442289). Wardle has said he did not invent the grid, and credit goes to a player (@irihapeta). — [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/wordle-online-word-game)
- UNLV researchers say sharing made the game communal, and that being able to share while keeping the answer secret drew people in. — [UNLV News](https://www.unlv.edu/news/article/word-wordle)
- A Mashable survey of 1,000+ players reportedly found that about 75% shared results to spark conversations with friends and family. — [DemandGen Report copy of Mashable](https://legacy.demandgenreport.com/the-wordle-hype-exploring-the-viral-sensation-behind-todays-hint-from-mashable) (S; secondary copy, original not retrieved)
- Brandwatch: people are likely more inclined to share good scores, but many players also honestly post failures. — [Brandwatch Bulletin](https://www.brandwatch.com/email/brandwatch-bulletin-0108-wordle/) (S)

### Inferences
- A friend leaderboard of about 100 people is still a big N. By the N-effect and local dominance, the motivating comparison is with the 3–5 friends nearest you in rank or start time, not your position out of 100. Displaying "you vs. the few people closest to you" should motivate more and sting less than a full ranked list.
- Rankings motivate the top and the competitive, and can drive away the bottom, the anxious and the uncompetitive. Med students are a high-achievement, high-comparison population who already live with class ranks. A game meant for fun should probably not add another visible rank by default.
- Wordle works because everyone played the same puzzle on the same day (identical conditions), the share is a playful artifact rather than a number, and posting is voluntary. Those three properties are the template.

### Gaps
- Christy & Fox (2014), "Leaderboards in a virtual classroom: A test of stereotype threat and social comparison explanations for women's math performance" (*Computers & Education* 78:66, doi 10.1016/j.compedu.2014.05.005), exists, but its findings could not be retrieved. Do not cite its results without checking.
- Hamari's 2014 review was found only as an abstract. Its leaderboard-specific findings were not extracted.
- No study was found comparing friend-only and global leaderboards head-to-head in a casual game. The friend > global claim rests on the social-psychology findings above, not on a direct A/B test.

## Q5. How games make comparison fair when players start at different times or in different conditions

### Takeaway
Common fairness tools are: (a) seasons or periodic resets that give everyone a common start; (b) normalizing by opportunity (count only active days, or give everyone the same number of "games"); (c) improvement-versus-self scoring; (d) cohorting with similar-level players (Duolingo's weekly ~30-person leagues); and (e) time-gated relationship levels rather than skill levels (Pokémon GO). Festinger's similarity hypothesis explains why these work: people want to compare with others in similar circumstances.

### Cited Findings
- Seasonal resets let everyone start over each season, which gives everyone an equal chance. The same product also normalizes by counting only the slots in which a member was active, so late joiners or paused users are not penalized. — [Guardey help: seasonal leaderboards](https://help.guardey.com/hc/en-us/articles/25681631983506-Using-seasonal-leaderboards) (product help page)
- Warzone's seasonal ladder gives everyone the same number of games at the start and adds games over time. Late joiners are penalized per missed game, so joining late to ride to a high rank does not pay. — [Warzone wiki: Seasonal Ladder](https://warzone.com/wiki/Seasonal_Ladder) (S)
- Without normalization, late joiners just have lower totals. One corporate-challenge tool tells admins to enroll everyone before launch. — [Vantage Fit help](https://www.vantagefit.io/en/help/getting-started/ts-joined-late-no-points/) (S)
- Players in a ladder community argued that resets matter because many people get a bad ranking, get discouraged and quit. — [SpringRTS forum](https://springrts.com/phpbb/viewtopic.php?p=261456) (forum opinion, S)
- Improvement-based framing: a contest template ranks people on beating their own previous month, not each other, which removes the late-joiner problem. — [MangoApps template](https://www.mangoapps.com/templates/contests/new-year-fresh-start) (S)
- Duolingo-style cohorting: weekly groups of up to about 30 learners at a similar level, with weekly promotion and demotion. — [Deconstructor of Fun](https://duolingo.deconstructoroffun.com/mechanics/leagues) (3P)
- Hay Day's derby is a weekly, team-based competition among neighborhoods, so the unit of comparison resets weekly and is collective. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/506627515) (S)
- Pokémon GO friendship tiers depend on days of interaction (1/7/30/90), not on either player's level, so a new player can become a "best friend" of a veteran. — [Bulbapedia](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Friends_(GO))
- Similarity hypothesis: people choose similar others as comparison targets because similarity makes the comparison informative. — [Wikipedia: Social comparison theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_comparison_theory)

### Inferences (mapped to the pizza game)
- "Day N since opening" is the right axis. Compare friends at the same game-day: show each friend's asset total at your current day N (e.g. "Day 21: me ₩X vs Minji ₩Y on her Day 21"), not their total today. A deterministic sim with a stored daily history makes this cheap.
- The neighborhood (campus/office/residential) is a condition, not a skill. Compare within the same neighborhood, or show neighborhood-specific badges ("캠퍼스 상권 1위"), so a campus shop is never ranked against a residential shop without context. This follows Festinger: compare like with like.
- Growth rate beats level. "This real week's growth %" (one real week = 7 game weeks) lets a late joiner top the board in week one. Weekly resets give a fresh start, and the losing week is forgotten.
- Same-start challenge: the sim is deterministic, so you can offer an optional "동기 개업" (same neighborhood + same start date seed) challenge where friends who opt in all start from identical conditions. Comparison is then perfectly fair, Wordle-style. This is an inference and design idea, not something found in the sources.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that tests "day-N normalization" or growth-rate leaderboards directly. Sources here are product help pages and forums.
- Official Duolingo documentation of league cohort matching was not retrieved.

## Q6. Lightweight social features that work without real-time multiplayer

### Takeaway
The proven async set is: view-only visits to a friend's space (Animal Crossing dreams), friends appearing as characters in your space (Restaurant City staff), small optional helping actions with caps (I Love Coffee 품앗이, Pokémon GO gifts), cooperative weekly goals in small groups (Hay Day derby, 30 people), and spoiler-free share artifacts (Wordle). Each works without the other person being online.

### Cited Findings
- View-only, host-absent visits: Animal Crossing's dream islands can be toured while the owner is offline, and visitors cannot change anything. — [Nintendo Support](https://en-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/50094/~/how-to-visit-another-players-dream-island-%28animal-crossing%3A-new-horizons%29)
- Friends as characters in your world: Restaurant City let you hire friends, even non-players, as staff. — [AOL/Joystiq guide, Dec 2009](https://www.aol.com/2009-12-29-restaurant-city-tips-and-cheats-getting-started-guide.html) (S)
- Capped helping visits: in 아이러브커피, visitors roast beans and sell coffee in a friend's café, capped per friend (3–4 times). — [나무위키](https://namu.wiki/w/아이러브커피) (W)
- Once-a-day gift per friend: Pokémon GO. — [Bulbapedia](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Friends_(GO))
- Small cooperative groups with weekly goals: Hay Day neighborhoods (≤30) and weekly derby, credited with retention by a former game lead. — [Wikipedia: Hay Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hay_Day) (W); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/506627515) (S)
- Shareable artifact: Wordle's generated, spoiler-free emoji grid. — [AP via Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/news/national-news/2022/01/04/he-made-wordle-for-his-partner-now-its-an-online-hit/)
- Personal expression as the long-lived core: 아이러브커피's interior themes and decoration were a reason for its longevity. — [나무위키](https://namu.wiki/w/아이러브커피) (W)

### Inferences
- Ranked from cheapest to richest for this game:
  1. Friend profile card: nickname, neighborhood, day N, a few badges and dex progress (dex/collection progress is non-zero-sum).
  2. View-only shop snapshot ("가게 구경"), rendered from the friend's deterministic state at their current day.
  3. One-tap emoji reactions (👍🍕🔥) left on the profile, positive-only, with no text, so there is no moderation burden.
  4. "Strategy peek": show which upgrades a friend bought by day N (a build order). This is the async version of copying a strategy, and it turns comparison into learning.
  5. Weekly friendly challenge, e.g. "this real week: highest growth % in the campus neighborhood", with category winners so several people can win.
  6. Cosmetic cameo: a friend's nickname appears as a customer in your shop. Restaurant City's charm with no economic link.
- None of these require both players to be online, and none create a resource the other person must send. That deliberately avoids Anipang hearts and FarmVille requests.

### Gaps
- No usage data (adoption or retention lift) was found for any specific lightweight feature, apart from the qualitative Hay Day retention attribution.
- No source was found on "copy a friend's strategy" mechanics in management games specifically.

## Q7. Privacy and comfort issues (embarrassment at low rank, wanting to hide numbers)

### Takeaway
The documented discomforts are: notification spam at odd hours, awkward social signals from unwanted contacts (Anipang), pressure and anxiety from visible rankings (Huschens, the maths-anxiety study), demotivation at the bottom, and selective sharing (people post good results more). Games that survived added opt-outs: refusing hearts, platform-level blocking, capped invites and league opt-outs.

### Cited Findings
- Anipang hearts arrived at midnight and 6 a.m., and many found this irritating. Receiving hearts from exes or not-close people created dilemmas about how to respond. — [머니투데이, 2012-09-22](https://www.mt.co.kr/tech/2012/09/22/2012092111361695742)
- Anipang allowed refusing hearts, and KakaoTalk allowed blocking app messages. Some users turned hearts off because of stress. — [매일신문, 2012-09-29](https://www.imaeil.com/page/view/2012092908273227181) (S)
- Kakao limited game invites to once a month (Jan 2013) because spam invites stressed users. — [매일신문, 2013-01-19](https://www.imaeil.com/page/view/2013011908354515957) (S)
- Rankings raise perceived pressure along with motivation. — [Huschens et al., HICSS 2019](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/items/7e2be796-8a77-4ac2-bfe5-8b851f39af88) (S)
- Anxious players dislike leaderboards regardless of rank. — [University of Galway, 2024](https://research.universityofgalway.ie/en/publications/not-only-amatter-ofposition-player-traits-which-influence-theexpe/) (S)
- Bottom performers reduce effort under relative scoring. — [LSE Research Online (Topcoder)](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/115983) (S)
- Third-party Duolingo guides say some learners find leagues stressful and turn them off to focus on their own progress. — [Duolingo Guides](https://duolingoguides.com/duolingo-turn-off-leagues/) (3P, unverified against official docs)
- Wordle sharing is voluntary, and people likely over-share good scores. — [Brandwatch](https://www.brandwatch.com/email/brandwatch-bulletin-0108-wordle/) (S)
- Animal Crossing's dream visits are view-only, so a visitor cannot damage or alter your space. — [Nintendo Support](https://en-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/50094/~/how-to-visit-another-players-dream-island-%28animal-crossing%3A-new-horizons%29)

### Inferences
- Asset totals are effectively a "wealth" number. Among classmates who already compete on grades, a raw ₩ figure next to your name invites judgement. Offer visibility levels: show the exact number, show only a tier or bracket (e.g. "🍕🍕🍕 성장 중"), or show nothing but badges. Default to the middle level.
- Never notify someone that they dropped in rank. Allow notifications only for positive, opt-in events (a friend reacted, a weekly challenge result), and respect quiet hours.
- Allow leaving a friend link without the other side being notified, and allow resetting or removing your profile from the friend list.

### Gaps
- No study was found specifically on hiding numeric wealth or assets in games. The recommendation is inferred from the ranking-pressure and anxiety findings.
- Strava, Apple Activity sharing and similar privacy-tier designs were not researched here.

## Q8. Concrete design lessons for the "compare shop assets with friends" feature

### Takeaway
Build it as a "friends' shops gallery with fair snapshots", not a "100-person wealth leaderboard". Compare at the same day N within the same neighborhood, foreground growth and badges, make visits view-only and reactions one-tap, keep Kakao for joining only, and give each player control over how exactly their numbers are shown.

### Cited Findings (the evidence each lesson rests on)
- Small, local comparison groups motivate more than large ones. — [Garcia & Tor 2009](https://public.websites.umich.edu/~smgarcia/pubs/n-effect.pdf); [Zell & Alicke via CRISP](https://crisp.org.uiowa.edu/sites/crisp.org.uiowa.edu/files/2020-04/20.5.pdf)
- People compare with similar others. — [Social comparison theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_comparison_theory)
- Leaderboards act as implicit hard goals. — [Landers et al. 2017](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/gamification-of-task-performance-with-leaderboards-a-goal-setting/)
- Low-ranked and anxious users can be demotivated. — [LSE/Topcoder](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/115983); [Galway 2024](https://research.universityofgalway.ie/en/publications/not-only-amatter-ofposition-player-traits-which-influence-theexpe/)
- Friend-gated progress and request spam cause backlash. — [Deceptive Patterns](https://deceptive.design/articles/farmville-friend-spam); [매일신문 2013](https://www.imaeil.com/page/view/2013011908354515957); [머니투데이 2012](https://www.mt.co.kr/tech/2012/09/22/2012092111361695742)
- View-only async visits and spoiler-free shares work. — [Nintendo](https://en-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/50094/~/how-to-visit-another-players-dream-island-%28animal-crossing%3A-new-horizons%29); [AP/Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/news/national-news/2022/01/04/he-made-wordle-for-his-partner-now-its-an-online-hit/)
- Friends as characters in your space created the warmth of Restaurant City. — [AOL 2009](https://www.aol.com/2009-12-29-restaurant-city-tips-and-cheats-getting-started-guide.html) (S)
- Self-expression (decoration) sustained 아이러브커피 for years. — [나무위키](https://namu.wiki/w/아이러브커피) (W)

### Inferences: design lessons (numbered, actionable)
1. **Compare at the same day N, not today.** The comparison view picks your current day N and shows each friend's asset total at their own day N, labelled "개업 N일차 기준". Friends who have not reached day N yet show "아직 N일 전" instead of a number. This is the single biggest fairness fix for different start dates.
2. **Neighborhood is a filter and a badge, not a footnote.** Default the comparison to friends in the same neighborhood. Mixed-neighborhood views show the neighborhood icon prominently, and per-neighborhood titles (캠퍼스 1위 / 오피스 1위 / 주거 1위) triple the number of winners.
3. **Show "near me", not "all 100".** Display the 2 friends just above and 2 just below you at the same day N, plus a "전체 보기" (see all) button. That is Yu-kai Chou's proximity and hope, and Zell & Alicke's local dominance, at once.
4. **Lead with growth and badges, keep the raw total secondary.** Headline metrics: "이번 주(현실 7일) 성장률", best single game-week, dex or recipe completion %, longest streak without stock-out. Several categories mean several people can be "best at something". The raw asset total appears in the profile, not as the headline rank.
5. **Weekly reset of the competitive layer.** One real week = 7 game weeks, a natural season. A small "이번 주 챔피언" board resets every real Monday, while all-time history lives in profiles and is never ranked.
6. **Optional same-start challenge (동기 개업).** Because the sim is deterministic, allow an opt-in challenge where participants share neighborhood and start-date seed. This is the only place where raw totals are ranked head-to-head, because conditions are identical (the Wordle principle).
7. **Visibility control per player.** Three levels: 정확한 금액 / 구간만(e.g. ₩1–5천만) / 숨김(뱃지만). Default to bracket. Never show "you dropped to #X". No push notifications about rank.
8. **Visits are view-only.** "가게 구경" renders a friend's shop snapshot, with no stealing, trash or rating that could be negative. Add one-tap positive emoji reactions (max 1 per friend per day, no counters shown publicly) so reactions can never become a popularity contest or an obligation streak.
9. **Never gate progress on friends.** No friend-consent items, no gifts needed to upgrade, no invite rewards (FarmVille and 아이러브커피 친구 동의서 are what to avoid). Social is pure bonus, and a solo player loses nothing.
10. **Kakao is for joining, not for nagging.** One share link or friend code to connect. No auto-messages into KakaoTalk, no "friend X beat you" pings, quiet hours if any notifications exist (Anipang's midnight hearts are what to avoid).
11. **A spoiler-free share card.** A generated image or emoji summary of "my shop this week" (neighborhood, day N, growth arrow, badge earned, pizza emojis for best-selling items) that players can choose to paste into a KakaoTalk group chat. A conversation starter, not a ranking (Wordle).
12. **Strategy peek turns envy into learning.** On a friend's profile, show their upgrade build order up to day N ("12일차에 오븐 2단계"). Comparing becomes "how did they do that?" instead of "I'm worse".
13. **Cosmetic friend cameos for warmth.** Optionally, friends' nicknames appear as occasional customers in your shop. This is Restaurant City's best-remembered idea, delivered with zero economic coupling.
14. **Expect most friends to be inactive at any time.** Design profiles to look fine when frozen (e.g. "마지막 영업: 3일 전"). Restaurant City's 90% drop shows how fast casual audiences thin. A friend list that shows a frozen shop politely is better than one that shows a "dead" friend at the bottom of a rank.

### Gaps
- These lessons have not been user-tested with the actual audience (Korean med students). A quick poll of 5–10 target players on the visibility default (exact / bracket / hidden) would validate lesson 7.
- No source directly tests day-N normalization or bracketed wealth display in games. Lessons 1 and 7 are inferences from social-comparison research and fairness patterns.
